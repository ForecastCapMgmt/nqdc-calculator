Defer, or take it now?

An educational illustration of one hypothetical election: defer pre-tax compensation inside a nonqualified plan, or take it as pay, pay tax, and invest the remainder in a taxable account. The figures update as the assumptions change. No signup is required.

Assumptions

Starting point: a higher tax rate now than at distribution, over seven years. Change any figure. These are not a forecast of your rates or returns.

The pre-tax compensation in this one illustration, from $5,000 to $500,000. It is the amount placed in the plan, not a suggestion of how much to defer. Type a figure or use the slider.

The combined rate assumed on this dollar if it is taken as pay now — whatever mix of federal, state, and other tax you want the illustration to use. Enter 45 for 45%, not 0.45. Allowed range: 20% to 60%. This is an assumption, not a tax calculation.

The rate assumed when the plan pays the balance out and it is taxed. The illustration is sensitive to this guess. Enter 35 for 35%. Allowed range: 20% to 60%.

How long the balance stays in the plan before it is paid and taxed in this illustration. Allowed range: 1 to 30 years.

Hypothetical annual growth of the deferred balance before the tax at distribution. In this model the plan balance is not taxed each year. Allowed range: 1% to 15%.

Hypothetical annual growth of money invested after paying tax on the compensation today. Enter an after-tax figure. The model compounds that rate as a constant. It does not itemize dividends, interest, or capital gains. Allowed range: 1% to 15%.

NQDC balances are unsecured promises of the employer. This selector names that credit risk. It does not change the dollar math, and it is not a rating of any company.
